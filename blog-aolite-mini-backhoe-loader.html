AOLITE Mini Backhoe Loaders: Where the "Mini" Label Starts and Stops on Our Line

"Do you make a mini backhoe loader?" I get this question every week, and the honest answer is a table, not a yes. Two machines on the AOLITE line wear the Mini badge — one electric, one diesel — and two more sit one step above them, called compacts, for reasons that matter more than the name. Here is the whole line from the factory side: what "mini" means when we say it, what each small machine can and cannot do, and how to match the label to the gate you have to drive it through.

Vivian — Foreign Trade Manager, AOLITE Heavy Industry
November 3, 2026 · 12 min read

Everything in this article hangs off one picture. Same badge on the hood, same factory, same warranty process — but the sizes, the jobs, and the sites they fit are completely different.

The full AOLITE backhoe loader line by operating weight. The two green bars are the Minis this article is about.

Before the details, one disclaimer I owe you: "mini" is not a protected word in this industry. There is no ISO line where a backhoe loader stops being mini, and different factories put the word on machines from under a tonne to over four. So when a buyer asks me for a mini, the first thing I do is ask what they need to fit through — because that number, not a marketing label, is what decides the machine.

"Do You Make a Mini Backhoe Loader?" — Why I Answer With a Table

If you search "mini backhoe loader" you will find the word used for machines that have almost nothing in common. One factory's mini is a 900 kg pedestrian machine; another's is a 4-tonne tractor-loader-backhoe with a cab and air conditioning. Both are telling the truth by their own definition, which is exactly the problem: the buyer is left comparing machines across a 4× weight range under one search term.

So here is our definition, stated plainly. On the AOLITE line, Mini means the BL 15-06 and the BL 20-07 — machines of 1,500 and 2,350 kg that are built to work inside and around buildings, on small farms, and in places a full-size backhoe loader simply cannot enter. Above them sit the BL 35-12 and BL 45-18 compacts, at 3,500 and 4,500 kg, which are small by the standards of construction machinery but are no longer door-and-corridor machines. Everything from the BL 70-25 up is a full-size machine, and we never call those mini.

I would rather draw you this line in the first email than discover in week three that your "mini" and mine were different machines. The table below is the one I actually send.

The BL 15-06 Mini: Our Electric Mini

The BL 15-06 Mini is the smallest backhoe loader we build, and the only electric one. It runs on a 72V or 120V battery pack driving an electric motor, weighs 1,500 kg, and measures 3,400 mm long, 1,200 mm wide and 2,175 mm tall. The loader carries 0.15 m³, the backhoe bucket 0.03 m³, and the machine digs to 1,580 mm and dumps at 1,738 mm. Travel is 12 km/h on 23×8.5-12 tyres — enough to cross a yard or a farm track, not to drive between towns.

What those numbers add up to is a machine for enclosed and emissions-sensitive work: inside livestock buildings, in greenhouses, in food-processing plants, in basements and parking levels, in historic city centres where a diesel engine is not welcome. No exhaust, and far less noise than a diesel of any size. On a farm it is the machine for daily small jobs — cleaning drainage ditches, digging post holes, loading feed — where firing up a 4-tonne machine for twenty minutes of work never made sense.

The honest limits are just as important. A 0.03 m³ backhoe bucket moves about a third of a standard builder's barrow per pass; 1,580 mm of dig depth reaches footing level on a shallow trench, not a pipe layer's trench. This is a machine that wins by being there, charged and quiet and small, not by moving metres of soil. If your day is measured in cubic metres dug, it is the wrong machine, and I will say so.

The BL 20-07 Mini: Our Diesel Mini

The BL 20-07 Mini is the same idea with a different heart: a Changchai 390 diesel putting out 25 kW at 2,400 rpm. It weighs 2,350 kg, digs to 1,850 mm, dumps at 2,000 mm, and carries a 0.4 m³ loader bucket with a 0.07 m³ backhoe bucket — roughly three times the backhoe volume of the 15-06. Transmission is stepless (CVT), which on a machine this size means smooth creeping forward while you trench, without hunting for a gear. Width is 1,180 mm on the narrow 650-16 tyre setup, or 1,450 mm on the wider option; length 4,700 mm, height 2,400 mm.

This is the mini for buyers who work outdoors, all day, away from power: smallholdings, landscaping crews, rental fleets serving residential clients, plumbing and utility contractors whose trenches are shallow but whose working days are long. You refuel it in a minute and run it for a shift, and the 18 km/h high range lets it follow a truck between nearby sites.

Its limits mirror its size. At 1,850 mm the dig depth is real digging but not deep utility work; the machine is stable for its class but you still respect it — a mini tipping on soft ground does the same damage to its operator as a big one. And the narrow tyres that let it fit a 1,180 mm gap are exactly the tyres you do not want in deep mud, which is why the wider option exists. Why One Mini Is Electric and the Other Is Not

Buyers often ask why our two minis split the power question between them instead of both going one way. The answer is that the two machines are aimed at two different working days.

The 15-06 is electric because the jobs it exists for are the jobs where electric wins outright: indoors, near livestock, near food, near people, and in short intense bursts with long gaps in between — the exact duty cycle a battery loves and a diesel hates. The 20-07 is diesel because its buyers need to run from morning to dark at sites with no grid, no charger and no patience for a machine that stops at 60%. Neither machine is the "better" one; they are two honest answers to two different sets of constraints.

If you are genuinely torn between them, the deciding questions are never about torque — they are about where the machine sleeps and whether there is a socket next to that spot. Where "Mini" Stops: the Two Compacts

Right above the minis sit the BL 35-12 and BL 45-18, and I want to be precise about why they are not on the mini page. It is not weight snobbery — it is three numbers that change what a machine can do.

  • Dig depth. 2,800 mm on the 35-12 and 3,000 mm on the 45-18, against 1,580 and 1,850 on the minis. That is the difference between digging to footing level and digging a utility trench to code.
  • Size. Both compacts are 5.3–5.6 metres long and 1.68–1.9 metres wide. They fit a farm track and a residential street; they do not fit a barn door or a corridor.
  • Buckets. 0.4–0.5 m³ on the loader and up to 0.25 m³ on the backhoe. Now you are moving material by the load, not by the barrow.

A large share of the buyers who first ask me for a "mini backhoe loader" actually end up in one of these two, because what they wanted was not smallness for its own sake — it was "not an 8-tonne machine". If you have a trailer, a yard to store it in, and trenches deeper than two metres, the compact is the honest answer, and no mini badge will change the physics.

The reverse is equally true, and it is why I keep both ends of the range. The buyer with a 1.2-metre gate does not want to hear that "the 35-12 is nearly as small". Nearly is the most expensive word in this business.

Turning Size Numbers Into Site Answers

A specification sheet is useless until you convert it into a yes or no about your own site. Here is how I do it with buyers, in the order that actually matters.

Width — against your narrowest gap

Measure the narrowest point the machine must pass: the farm gate, the side-of-house passage, the yard arch, the elevator door. Then compare: BL 15-06 at 1,200 mm, BL 20-07 at 1,180 mm (narrow tyres) or 1,450 mm, BL 35-12 at 1,680 mm. Leave working margin — a machine steered by a nervous operator needs more than its catalogue width. If the gap is under 1.1 metres, even our minis need the passage widened, and it is better to learn that now than on delivery day.

Height — against your lowest beam

The 15-06 stands 2,175 mm tall, the 20-07 2,400 mm. Indoors, the number to check is not the ceiling but the lowest pipe, cable tray or doorframe on the route — and remember the backhoe boom rises above the machine's parked height when you swing it. If the work is indoors and the clearance is under 2.3 metres, we measure the route together before anything else.

Length — against your transport

3,400 mm and 4,700 mm respectively, against 5.3–5.6 m for the compacts. That length decides what trailer you need, whether the machine rides legally with the boom overhanging, and how the container gets loaded. The minis' small dimensions are also why they are the cheapest machines on our line to move — for the shipping arithmetic, the shipping guide walks through container loading with real numbers.

Dig depth — against your actual work

Compare your deepest recurring job, not your deepest imaginable one, with the figures in the table above — and ask me for each model's measuring datum, because where "maximum dig depth" is measured from varies between factories, and a 200 mm disagreement in the datum can quietly erase a machine's advantage. If you want the full method for reading weight and dimension figures, the weight and specs article covers it in detail.

How to Ask for an AOLITE Mini Without Getting the Wrong Machine

Because "mini" means different things to different factories, a quotation built on the word alone is a lottery. Send these six lines with your first enquiry — about any machine, from any factory, including ours — and the quotation that comes back will be for the machine you actually meant:

  1. The narrowest gap the machine must pass through, in millimetres, and whether it can be widened.
  2. The deepest recurring job, in metres — not the deepest one-off.
  3. Hours per day the machine will actually work, and whether it is bursts or continuous.
  4. Power at the machine's sleeping spot: grid and socket, or nothing.
  5. Ground and weather: hard standing, mud, slopes, and how hot the summers get.
  6. Your country's rules: emission stage, and whether electric machines get any local advantage.

Line 6 matters more than buyers expect. A diesel mini still carries an engine with an emission stage attached, and the paper trail for a small machine crosses the same border as the paper trail for a big one — the emissions and certificates article covers what your port will actually ask for.

The Short Version

Yes, AOLITE makes a mini backhoe loader — two of them. The BL 15-06 Mini is the electric one: 1,500 kg, 1,200 mm wide, silent and emission-free, for indoor and close-quarters work. The BL 20-07 Mini is the diesel one: 2,350 kg, 25 kW, CVT, for long outdoor days far from a socket. Above them the BL 35-12 and BL 45-18 compacts take over at 3–3.5 metres of dig depth, and from the BL 70-25 upward the line is full-size.

The label is the beginning of the conversation, not the end of it. Bring me your narrowest gate, your deepest recurring trench and your machine's sleeping spot, and the line above will point at exactly one row — and I will tell you honestly if that row is one we do not build.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does AOLITE make a mini backhoe loader?
Yes — two. The BL 15-06 Mini is electric (72V/120V battery, 1,500 kg operating weight, 1,580 mm dig depth) and the BL 20-07 Mini is diesel (Changchai 390, 25 kW, 2,350 kg, 1,850 mm dig depth). Above them, the BL 35-12 and BL 45-18 compacts at 3,500 and 4,500 kg cover the 2.8–3.0 m dig-depth range. Everything from the BL 70-25 upward is full-size.
Is a mini backhoe loader the same thing as a mini excavator?
No, and mixing them up wastes everyone's time. A mini backhoe loader is still a backhoe loader: a loader bucket at the front, a digging arm at the rear, on wheels, steered and driven like a tractor. A mini excavator is a tracked machine that digs from one position and swings. If your work is "dig, then carry the material somewhere", that is backhoe-loader work; if it is "dig deep in one spot all day", a mini excavator may serve you better. Tell me the job and I will tell you which machine family it belongs to — even when the answer is not one we build.
How deep can the AOLITE minis dig?
The BL 15-06 Mini digs to 1,580 mm and the BL 20-07 Mini to 1,850 mm — footing-level and shallow-service-trench territory. If you need to go to 2.8 or 3.0 metres, that is the BL 35-12 or BL 45-18 compact. Note that maximum dig depth is quoted from a stated datum that varies between factories, so compare depths only after confirming where each figure is measured from.
Can an AOLITE mini fit through a standard gate or doorway?
Depends on the gap, and we check it before anything else. The BL 15-06 Mini is 1,200 mm wide and 2,175 mm tall; the BL 20-07 Mini is 1,180 mm wide on narrow tyres (1,450 mm on the wider option) and 2,400 mm tall. A single-leaf door at 900 mm will stop either machine; a double gate or garage opening at 2.4 metres passes both with margin. Always allow working margin beyond the catalogue width for steering.
Which mini should I choose — electric or diesel?
Choose by where the machine sleeps and how it works, not by torque figures. Short bursts indoors or near livestock, with a socket where it parks: the electric BL 15-06. Long days outdoors at sites with no power: the diesel BL 20-07. I wrote the full decision framework, including running costs, in the electric versus diesel mini article — read that before any brochure.
Are mini backhoe loaders cheaper to ship than full-size machines?
Generally yes, because shipping cost tracks size and loading complexity, and the minis are the smallest machines on our line: 3.4–4.7 m long and 1.18–1.2 m wide. But the exact container plan depends on your port pair and the configuration, so rather than a rule of thumb, ask me for the loading plan with your enquiry — it is part of the quotation, not an extra.
